# Market scanner

Scans the liquid US stock market every night, ranks what is trending, shows which Shariah-listed stocks the fixed rules would hold, and logs those picks so their later returns can be checked. The scanner places no orders itself. It cancels a queued stock buy when serious news breaks before the open, and the autopilot trades its picks only while the sleeve is switched on, which it is not by default. Plan and phases: [scanner-plan.md](scanner-plan.md); evidence behind every number: [scanner-research.md](scanner-research.md).

**Data.** At 00:01 UTC, and once at startup if that run was missed, the scanner reads Alpaca's active US equities, keeps the tradable ones on NASDAQ, NYSE, ARCA, AMEX and BATS with plain tickers (12,745 today), downloads 45 days of adjusted daily bars for all of them, and calls a stock liquid at \$10 or more with \$20M or more traded a day (2,645). It keeps eight years of adjusted daily bars for every liquid and every Shariah-listed stock in `data/scanner/bars.parquet` (4.6M rows, 78 MB). The first build took 6 minutes and the nightly refresh takes about 70 seconds; requests run four at a time and the panel is saved every 500 symbols, so an interrupted build resumes. A refresh that failed, or that the machine slept through, is repeated by a check every 10 minutes, up to 6 failures a day. Alpaca rescales past prices when a stock splits or pays a dividend, so each night's window is compared with what is stored: a symbol that no longer matches within 0.01%, or whose window no longer touches its stored history, is fetched again in full, and a symbol Alpaca refuses is left out instead of kept wrong. Today's session is never stored while it is open. Delisted stocks are not available from Alpaca, so every backtest here is survivorship-biased.

**Shariah lists.** SPUS holdings are downloaded from SP Funds, cleaned (cash, CUSIP-style ids and CVRs dropped, class shares written the way Alpaca writes them) and kept as dated copies in `data/scanner/lists/`. A download that is not a list, or that lost more than half its names, is refused and the last good copy stays in use. A list older than 7 days stops counting toward picks. HLAL's holdings are a Google Sheet that some networks block: save its CSV as `data/scanner/lists/HLAL.csv` (a Ticker, Symbol or StockTicker column) and it is used the same way. sp-funds.com answers 403 to Python's default client name and 200 to a plain descriptive one, so the scanner sends `algotr-scanner/0.1` and does not imitate a browser. The list is the authority for trading; the scanner does not screen companies itself.

**Ranking.** Each stock gets seven figures per session, all from data up to that session (a test cuts the history at several dates and checks that nothing changes): the 200-day trend, 12-1 and 6-1 momentum, Clenow's regression score (checked against a scipy fit), residual 12-1 momentum (the return left after the equal-weight market's part is taken out, summed over its standard deviation), 20-day dollar volume and the largest one-day move in 90 sessions. A stock qualifies when it is liquid, above its 200-day average, has a year of history and no 15% single-day move. Ten variants are fixed in advance, five scores (12-1, 6-1, trend strength, the rank average of the first two, residual 12-1) with the top 10 or top 20 of the listed names, weighted by inverse volatility with no name above 10%. Re-ranking is monthly and a name that stops qualifying is dropped at the next weekly check. Every variant is counted in the Deflated Sharpe, so adding one later makes the bar higher.

**Judging.** `POST /scanner/evaluate` runs the ten variants over the stored history and judges them together with the same five checks as the strategies, set against the same stocks held equally without picking. On the data of 12 November 2026 (from June 2020) none of the ten beats that benchmark's Sharpe ratio (best 1.05 for Clenow's top 10, against 1.19; the residual-momentum variants made 0.93 and 0.90), none reaches a 95% Deflated Sharpe (best 86%), and the probability of backtest overfitting is 74%. Read that with the caveats the result carries: both the lists and the data are survivorship-biased. So the scanner stays a view and a log; promoting it to a trading sleeve needs the checks to pass and 8 weeks of shadow results.

**Does the veto work?** `POST /scanner/vetoes` runs an event study on the stored history (`vetoes.py`). Every time a name was vetoed (a serious negative headline or a pending deal, tagged to at most three names, in the last five days, the rule a new buy meets) while the scanner would have considered it (listed, liquid, trending), its return from the next session's open, where an order placed after the close fills, to the close 1, 5 and 21 sessions on is set against the same session's other considered names that were not vetoed. Excess is averaged within a session before it is averaged across sessions, because names on one day move together. On 12 November 2026: 458 events on 128 names since 2018, excess return +0.01% at one session (95% interval -0.17% to +0.20%), -0.03% at five (-0.43% to +0.36%) and -0.44% at 21 (-1.34% to +0.46%), vetoed names below their peers 49%, 51% and 53% of the time. The veto does not show the protection it is there to give, and the data rules out a large one (more than about 0.4% over a week or 1.3% over a month); a small one it cannot see. It binds about 57 times a year, so it costs little to keep, but it is insurance, not an edge, and a wider use (cutting size, not only blocking buys) has no support from this. By kind, guidance cuts and regulatory news are on the weak side and legal news on the strong side at 21 sessions, on 117, 160 and 73 events, which is noise at that size. The lists are today's and delisted names are missing, which flatters the names that stayed, vetoed or not; the result is written to the audit log (`veto-study`) and can be rerun as headlines accumulate.

**Shadow log.** Each refresh writes one `scan` entry per session to the hash-chained audit log with the picks of all ten variants, the same-day benchmark names and a fingerprint of the prices seen. Later, `GET /scanner/shadow` computes each pick's 1, 5 and 21 session returns from stored prices against SPUS and against the listed universe. Proving an edge this way takes years, so treat it as an integrity check and an early warning.

**News and events.** Every 15 minutes, and once after each refresh, the scanner fetches Alpaca's news (Benzinga and fiscal.ai) for the Shariah-listed names and every stock shown as a candidate (259 today) into `data/scanner/news.sqlite`. The first poll for a name reaches back 60 days (10,492 articles, about a minute), so a name added later fills in on its own. Ordered rules on the wording of each headline give it a kind, a direction and a severity: a guidance cut, a halt, an equity offering, a regulator action, a recall or a downgrade, a results beat or miss, and so on. An article tagged to six or more stocks is market commentary. A **veto** is a high-severity negative event, or a pending takeover that pins the price, in an article tagged to three stocks or fewer in the last 5 days; the stock then fails "No serious setback in the news in 5 days" and shows the headline. In the 30 days to 3 November, 29 of 7,333 headlines were vetoes (19 guidance cuts). The rules are patterns, not understanding: a startup's bankruptcy in an article that names a followed company still vetoes it, so the headline is always shown. News is a veto and a flag, not a ranking input, and no return is claimed for it. Near-duplicates are not merged: in 30 days only 11 of 3,701 articles were a second feed's copy, while a similarity cut-off also merged 257 notes from different brokers.

**Sentiment.** After each poll FinBERT scores every stored headline in the FinRL sidecar and the record (score from -1 to +1, confidence, a risk from 1 to 5 read from the rule typing, the model and a hash of the text) is stored beside it and shown in the headline table. It does not veto or rank, and it reaches trading only as two of the AI agents' features ([ai.md](ai.md#from-research-to-orders)): on 10,010 headlines it tracked the same day's move (rank correlation +0.09) and not the first tradable day's (+0.01), and its confidence is poorly calibrated. Weights are non-commercial (CC BY-NC-SA). Setup, the record and the evidence: [sentiment.md](sentiment.md), [sentiment-research.md](sentiment-research.md).

**SEC filings.** Only when `SEC_CONTACT` is set in `.env`; the SEC asks every client to name a contact, and the scanner does not use one it was not given. Without it the scanner runs on news alone and the status says so. With it, the 8-K item numbers of each followed company come from data.sec.gov at no more than 10 requests a second. Items 1.03 (bankruptcy), 3.01 (delisting notice) and 4.02 (financials not to be relied on) are vetoes; 2.04, 2.05, 2.06 and 1.02 are flagged; and item 2.02 (results) gives the days since the last results and an estimate of the next from the median gap between earlier ones, shown as "Results not due within a week". There is no earnings calendar: the date is an estimate and is labelled as one.

**Pre-open review.** A job checks every 5 minutes whether the market opens within 45 minutes (Alpaca's calendar, so holidays and early closes count). Once a day it looks at every open stock buy order, polls the news for those names, and cancels an order when a veto headline appeared after the order was placed. A review that checked any order writes a `review` entry to the audit log with what it cancelled and why; `GET /scanner/review` returns the last one.

**Crypto.** A separate table that only displays: nothing in it is ever traded. The coins are the list in `CRYPTO_COINS` in `.env`, ten established ones by default (BTC, ETH, SOL, XRP, LINK, AVAX, DOT, LTC, BCH, UNI), which is a convenience and not a Shariah ruling. Their daily bars against USD (four years, every day of the week) are kept in `data/scanner/crypto.parquet`, a file of its own because weekends would put empty rows into the stock panel's windows. A check every hour refreshes the whole file when yesterday's bar is missing (2.5 seconds, 0.6 MB); a failure keeps the last good file and shows a warning. Coins are ranked by 4 week return and show the 1 and 13 week returns and the price against its 200-day average. Those horizons follow the published evidence on crypto momentum, which is at 1 to 4 weeks (Liu, Tsyvinski and Wu, 2022), not the equity 12-1 windows. A coin with under 365 days of history or none from Alpaca is left out with the reason, and the stablecoins USDT, USDC and USDG and the gold token PAXG are never ranked. There is no dollar-volume floor, because Alpaca's own volumes are small (Bitcoin's median is about \$120,000 a day).

**Sleeve.** The autopilot can hold a small share of the scanner's picks. It is off by default, and while it is off the plan is exactly what it was (a test pins that). `PUT /systematic/automation` takes `sleeve: {mode, share, variant, override}`. `mode` is `off`, `shadow` (the plan shows the orders the sleeve would place and places none) or `trade`; `share` is a fraction of the autopilot's allocation, at most 25%; `variant` is one of the eight. The sleeve holds the variant's scheduled names: those from the last month-end ranking, less any dropped at a weekly check (`GET /scanner/sleeve`). Trading needs a record: the variant's five checks from an evaluation no older than 30 days, 40 logged sessions and an intact audit chain. Today the record fails (Sharpe below the benchmark's, Deflated Sharpe under 95%, one session logged), so `trade` places nothing until it passes or the override is switched on, and an override labels the plan and the page "Trading, failed checks". While it trades, the sleeve takes its share before the strategies do (20% of a 10% allocation is 2% of the account) and places fractional `day` market orders through Alpaca even when the strategies use Nautilus, which sizes whole shares. A run starts at most 3 new names and adds nothing to a name with a serious setback in the news. Data older than 36 hours, a last session more than 5 days old, a list more than 7 days old or a failed refresh freeze the sleeve where it stands: it never sells for a stale scan or a failed record. It sells when switched off (what it bought is remembered separately from the strategies' symbols), when the drawdown stop fires, or when a name leaves the schedule. The scanner hands its picks over through the `SleeveProvider` contract in `core/sleeve.py`, so the autopilot does not import it.

| Endpoint | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `GET /scanner/status` | data state, progress while refreshing, the funnel and the health of each list |
| `POST /scanner/run` | refresh in the background (202; 409 if one is running) |
| `GET /scanner/latest` | today's ranking across the market, the picks of every variant, the funnel |
| `GET /scanner/symbol/{symbol}` | every test a stock must pass, with its figure, and the verdict |
| `GET /scanner/shadow` | forward returns of the logged picks |
| `POST /scanner/evaluate`, `GET /scanner/evaluation` | judge the ten variants; the latest result |
| `POST /scanner/vetoes`, `GET /scanner/vetoes` | test the news veto on the stored headlines and prices; the latest result |
| `GET /scanner/news/{symbol}` | the stock's newest headlines with their type and sentiment record, the 3 day badge and the SEC summary |
| `GET /scanner/review` | the last pre-open review: orders checked and cancelled |
| `GET /scanner/sleeve?variant=` | the variant's scheduled holdings, the names the news blocks, the promotion record and any data problem |
| `GET /scanner/crypto` | the listed coins by 4 week return with the 1 and 13 week returns and the 200 day trend, and the coins left out |

Market-data clients (`core/broker.py`) carry a 60 second timeout and 3 retries, because `requests` waits forever by default: the first live build hung on a silently dropped connection. The trading client is left alone, since a retried order could be placed twice.

## Where the code is

`src/app/features/scanner/`, built on `src/app/core/` (the audit log, the Alpaca clients) and `src/app/quant/` (performance metrics and significance tests); see [architecture.md](architecture.md).

| Module | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| `universe.py` | Alpaca's assets to tradable and liquid stocks; the names file |
| `lists.py` | the Shariah list sources, parsing, dated copies and staleness |
| `bars.py` | adjusted daily bars, batched and fetched four requests at a time |
| `store.py` | the Parquet panel, the adjusted-price check and the merge |
| `refresh.py` | one refresh: lists, the census of the market, the missing history |
| `features.py`, `rank.py` | the seven features; eligibility, the ten variants, weights and the monthly and weekly schedule |

| `scan.py` | today's ranking, the picks and the reasons for each stock |
| `shadow.py` | the log of picks and their forward returns |
| `headlines.py` | the rules that type a headline, and the veto |
| `news.py` | the SQLite store of headlines, the Alpaca feed and the 3 day badge |
| `sentiment.py` | FinBERT's record for each headline and the scorer that keeps it ([sentiment.md](sentiment.md)) |
| `filings.py` | SEC 8-K items, the results cadence and the EDGAR client |

| `service.py` | `Scanner`: state, the nightly job, and the calls the router makes |
| `router.py`, `schemas.py` | the HTTP routes and models |
