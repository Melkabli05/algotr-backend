# Systematic strategies and autopilot

Long-only, cash, daily-bar strategies with fixed published rules (nothing is fitted). Research and the reasoning behind them are in [quant-research.md](quant-research.md). The five below are the built-in templates; strategies are managed on the Strategies page (see [Managing strategies](#managing-strategies)).

| Strategy | Rule | Universe |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `halal-etf-trend` (default) | 200-day trend per ETF, equal sleeves | SPUS, HLAL |
| `halal-multi-trend` | each ETF held by the share of five trend windows (50 to 250 days) it is above, sized to 12% annual volatility and never above 1x, moving a quarter of the way to each target weight every day | SPUS, HLAL |
| `halal-etf-rotation` | dual momentum: the two best 6-month performers that gained, else cash | SPUS, HLAL, UMMA, SPSK |
| `btc-trend-200` | hold while the close is above the 200-day average, else cash | BTC/USD |
| `crypto-donchian` | nine Donchian breakout models vote, basket sized to 25% volatility, capped at 1x | BTC/USD, ETH/USD |

**When a trade fills.** A backtest fills each trade where the live one fills. Stock orders are placed at 00:05 UTC and wait for the next open, so the ETF rules buy and sell at the next day's open: the night after a signal still earns what was held before (`fills="next_open"`, opens from the same adjusted bars). Alpaca's crypto day bars close at 00:00 UTC and the coin orders go out five minutes later, so the crypto rules fill at the close they decided on (`fills="close"`). The rule lab, the drift monitor's strategy streams and the scanner's variants use the same rule; the agents' judgement keeps close fills on both sides, because their environment trades at the close.

Every strategy is judged by five checks (Sharpe above buy and hold, smaller max drawdown, profitable in the last 30%, profitable at 50 bps per trade, and a Deflated Sharpe Ratio of at least 95%: the chance the edge is real after allowing for skew, fat tails and the luck of picking the best of the strategies tried, per Bailey and López de Prado). The trials are every rule and every lab variant ever tried (`lab.search`): the five rules, the other trend windows, the rotation settings and the multi-speed rule's volatility targets and speeds, 24 today, plus any new variant the audit log shows the lab ran. A variant counts by its label, so a field added to the spec does not count it again. The luck bar spreads the trials' Sharpe ratios by the measured spread of the family's variants in the latest lab run, but never by less than one standard error, so a family of near-identical variants cannot lower it. "Sharpe above buy and hold" is a test, not a comparison of two numbers: the lead must have a p-value under 10% by the stationary bootstrap of the Sharpe difference (Ledoit and Wolf, 2008; block length by Politis and White, both from `arch`).

**Multi-speed trend.** `halal-multi-trend` is the 200-day rule's more careful sibling and runs beside it. Its exposure to a fund is the share of five trend windows (50, 100, 150, 200 and 250 days) the close is above, so a single cross moves it a fifth and not all the way; the basket is then scaled to 12% annual volatility from the last 60 days (capped at 1x, nothing borrowed); and each weight moves a quarter of the way to that aim every day (`partial_adjustment`, an exponentially weighted average of the aims, which is the form Gârleanu and Pedersen derive for trading costs: slower signals are traded toward, not jumped to). The windows, the target and the rate come from the trend literature ([strategy-research.md](strategy-research.md)) and were not tuned to the ETFs' five years. Its neighbours (10% and 15% volatility, faster and slower speeds, a 50% rate, and a 5% no-trade band instead of the rate) are run by the rule lab and count as trials; their Sharpe ratios sit between 0.84 and 0.88, so none is worth tuning to. On its own history (from March 2021) it has a Sharpe of 0.91 and a drawdown of -14% against 0.87 and -25% for holding both funds, at 3.3 units of turnover a year (the band version took 6.0, the 200-day rule takes 4.7), and a CAGR of 9.0% against 15.2% for holding; against the 200-day rule it gives up return (CAGR 9.0% against 15.0%, Sharpe 0.91 against 1.12) for a smaller drawdown (-14% against -20%), as the century of data says it should: it is a risk rule, and its case rests on the 1926 proxy (Sharpe 0.88 against the market's 0.63, p 0.1%, drawdown -30% against -84%) and on the bear markets these five years did not have. Widening the universe to the sukuk and REIT funds (SPSK, SPRE) was measured and left out: over 2021 to 2026 they lost money held (0.9% and -2.5% a year) and dragged any blend down, which says more about those years than about diversification. UMMA and SPWO start in January 2022 and December 2023, too late to leave a window to test on.

**A long history for the significance tests.** Two of the checks are statistical tests whose power grows with the length of the sample, and SPUS and HLAL have about five years after the 200-day warm-up, dominated by 2020 and 2022. On that alone the ETF trend rule cannot be told from luck (p 29%, deflated Sharpe 79% today). So a rule marked `proxy` (the ETF trend) runs the two tests on the US stock market's daily total return since 1926 from Ken French's data library, the way trend research is usually tested: there the rule's Sharpe is 0.81 against buy and hold's 0.63 with half the drawdown, p 2.2%, and a deflated Sharpe of 100% after 17 trials. The proxy has no opens, so it fills a day late, at the next close, which errs against the rule; cash earns nothing, as it does here. The other three checks (drawdown, the last 30%, 50 bps) stay on the ETFs' own prices, which is what costs and recent behaviour are about. The proxy is the whole US market, not a Shariah index: it says the rule works on US equities over a century, not that SPUS will. The file is fetched at start and refreshed when older than five weeks (`data/proxy/us-market.csv`); without it the tests run on the ETFs' own history and the rule fails them. The rotation and crypto rules have no proxy: rotation needs its four ETFs, and crypto has no long history. Today the ETF trend passes all five and the other three fail. Capital is split between running strategies by equal risk contribution (risk parity): each adds the same share of risk, measured from the covariance of the strategies' own returns over the last year (a rule that sat in cash all year is sized by its universe, which is the risk it takes when it buys; coins' weekends fold into Monday). Correlation counts, so two strategies that move together share one budget. The solver is SciPy's on Spinu's convex form; skfolio has it too but needs cvxpy and scikit-learn for at most four weights. Volatility targeting was measured and rejected for the long-only ETF rules: it cut drawdown but also return, with no Sharpe gain. Crypto strategies carry a note: scholarly opinion on crypto is divided, so check it is acceptable to you. Verify the ETFs' screening with their issuers.

**Event-driven check.** The rule lab screens every variant with the fast vectorised backtest; a trend rule can then be run through NautilusTrader's own event-driven backtest (`POST /systematic/validate/{name}`, the Research page's "Run in Nautilus"). There the rule (`trading_node/strategies/trend.py`) decides on each day's closes once every asset's bar is in, sends market orders that fill at the next bar's open, trades whole shares (a millionth of a coin), pays its fee on each fill, and leaves changes inside the 5% band alone, all out of one account. The result sits beside the vectorised one over the same days. On today's data the ETF trend rule differs by 0.17 points of CAGR a year (14.19% against 14.36%, 58 orders, $2,038 of fees on $100,000) and the Bitcoin trend by 0.02, so the fast engine's simplifications cost little. Each check is written to the audit log (`event-check`). The rotation and Donchian rules are not ported.

**Strategy lab and audit.** The Research page's Rule lab section (or `POST /systematic/lab`) tests every variant of every rule at once: the 100 to 250 day trend windows, the rotation windows and selection sizes. Each rule family runs in its own process (`ProcessPoolExecutor`, `spawn`; one failing family never stops the others; about 4 seconds for all three on 3 workers instead of 12). Variants are judged together, because picking the best of many inflates results: a Romano-Wolf step-down on arch's `SPA` (does anything beat holding cash after the search?), the Deflated Sharpe Ratio counted over every distinct variant *ever* tried (repeats are not double counted), the Harvey-Liu-Zhu t-statistic hurdle of 3, and the Probability of Backtest Overfitting by combinatorially symmetric cross-validation. A high overfitting probability means the ranking between variants is noise, so do not tune the settings. The lab never changes what autopilot trades. (arch's own `StepM` fails once its candidate set shrinks, so the step-down is our own loop over `SPA`.) Candidates should be comparable to each other and to the benchmark: SPA is not scale-invariant.

**FinRL, in its own environment.** [FinRL](https://github.com/AI4Finance-Foundation/FinRL) cannot be installed beside this project (its GitHub master pins `alpaca-py<0.38` and `websockets==10.4`), so it runs as a sidecar: `uv run poe finrl-setup` creates `finrl_sidecar/.venv` and installs FinRL at one pinned commit (`7e98df4`, see `finrl_sidecar/requirements.txt`). Each job is a subprocess (`finrl_sidecar/runner.py`) that gets the prices as files and a scrubbed environment: no Alpaca key, no secret, no network use by our code. The scanner's headline sentiment runs in the same environment, from weights fetched by `uv run poe finbert-setup` ([sentiment.md](sentiment.md)), and so do the SHAP explanations of the agents' decisions ([xai.md](xai.md)). "Run FinRL" (or `POST /systematic/lab/finrl`) runs every FinRL feature that fits a long-only Shariah daily-bar system and judges each by the same five gates as everything else, with the audit log recording the FinRL commit, the package versions and every trial: `FeatureEngineer` (technical indicators and the turbulence index), the stock-trading environment with all five SB3 agents (A2C, DDPG, PPO, SAC, TD3, three seeds each over anchored walk-forward folds), the ensemble agent (rolling retraining, best validation Sharpe trades), the portfolio-allocation environment, the portfolio-optimisation environment with the EIIE agent, Optuna tuning, and pyfolio backtest statistics. Three things are deliberately not used, and the dashboard says why for each: the Alpaca paper/live trading module (it would place orders around our safeguards: drawdown halt, no shorts, audit, Nautilus risk checks; orders go through autopilot only), the foreign data processors (they need accounts outside our universe; prices come from Alpaca through the audited loader), and the crypto environments (crypto stays off by default). Two features cannot run on current dependencies and are marked unavailable with the exact error: the RLlib backend (upstream imports an A2C algorithm that current Ray removed) and the ElegantRL backend (its own training loop fails on current PyTorch with "Can't detach views in-place", and the crashed worker leaves the parent waiting, which is why every sidecar job also has a hard time limit). Two upstream bugs are worked around inside the sidecar and documented in `runner.py`: the ensemble agent loops over models it has no parameters for, and the tuner imports a `utils` module FinRL does not ship. FinRL's own tuner scores trials on whatever "trade" environment it is given, which would be data snooping on the test period, so ours scores on a validation window before it. The gate for data a model never saw compares out-of-sample Sharpe with a training figure, so every feature reports one: the agents and the portfolio environments their Sharpe over the training window, EIIE the same, and the ensemble the mean validation Sharpe of the model it chose for each window (the score it was picked on, so the nearest thing to training it has). Seeds and trials are counted in the Deflated Sharpe, FinRL's results never change what autopilot trades, and a feature only becomes eligible by passing every gate.

**AI agents and drift.** PPO, SAC and A2C agents that choose weights over SPUS, HLAL and cash, a UCB1 ensemble over them and a drift monitor over every strategy are in [ai.md](ai.md); the research behind them, and what they measured, is in [ml-research.md](ml-research.md). The agents hold a pretend portfolio; their UCB1 ensemble trades only once it passes the gates, as the strategy `ai-ensemble`. The drift monitor can cut a strategy's size: the autopilot's plan multiplies each strategy's share by the monitor's `scale`.

Every autopilot run and every lab run is appended to `data/audit.jsonl`, a hash-chained log: each entry stores the hash of the one before it, so an entry edited, removed or reordered anywhere but the end breaks the chain (`GET /systematic/audit` and the dashboard show whether it is intact). An intact chain does not prove the contents are true, and it cannot see entries cut off the end or a file rewritten with every hash recomputed; keep a copy of the latest hash elsewhere if you need that. Each entry carries a manifest that makes a run repeatable: a SHA-256 of the configuration, a fingerprint of the exact prices seen (span and a hash of every value), the git commit and whether there were uncommitted changes, the library versions and the seed. Autopilot entries also record a compliance check of the account: no short positions and no borrowed cash.

**Autopilot** (APScheduler) rebalances daily at 00:05 UTC, once yesterday's bars are complete, and catches up when a day was missed or its run failed, for instance because the machine woke offline: at startup, then every 10 minutes, up to 6 failures a day. `dry-run` records the plan only; `paper` also places market orders. Safeguards: paper accounts only, never shorts or borrows, skips while the Nautilus node runs, and a drawdown stop on the autopilot's own book (below). By default the strategies are chosen automatically, with hysteresis: once a day each strategy's checks are judged, a strategy starts after passing on 10 days in a row and stops after failing on 10, and a pass or a fail in between restarts the count (`streak` in the overview; the strategy table shows it). The first judgement starts from what the last run traded. Crypto strategies run only when you switch "Include crypto" on, and picking strategies by hand in Settings bypasses the checks. The allocation is split between the running strategies by risk (above); symbols the autopilot bought are sold when no selected strategy wants them. State and the last 30 runs persist in `data/automation.json`. Stock orders placed while the market is closed queue for the next open.

**Fill costs.** Every order the autopilot sends keeps its decision price (the close the signal used). Once it is done (each run, and the 10-minute check between runs, reads it back by Alpaca's id or by the client id Nautilus gave it), the run record gets the fill price and time and two costs in basis points, positive when they cost money: `cost_bps`, the fill against the price the backtest fills at (the session's open for a stock, the decision price for a coin, plus Alpaca's 0.25% coin fee, charged in coin and not in the price), which is what the backtest's `fee_bps` has to cover; and `shortfall_bps`, the fill against the decision price, the night included. The Recent runs panel shows the notional-weighted cost per asset class against the `fee_bps` the rules assume (5 for the ETFs, 25 for crypto). A stock limit that the open gapped past settles unfilled. The record covers the last 30 runs; the audit log keeps every run.

**Drawdown.** The stop watches what the autopilot holds, not the account. Each strategy (and the scanner sleeve while it trades) has a book: the capital the plan gave it, the quantities it meant to hold at the prices it planned them at, and a value index that moves only with those holdings' prices. The whole autopilot's index is every book's gain over every book's capital. So a deposit, a withdrawal or a trade by hand in the same account never moves the drawdown, and neither does an allocation of 99% versus 10%. A strategy trades at half size while its own book, or the whole autopilot, is at least `reduce_at` of the limit below its peak (50% of the limit by default; 100% switches it off), and back at full size once it recovers. From three quarters of the limit the autopilot only sells and no book grows (`defensive`, one of the kill switch's five steps in [compliance.md](compliance.md)). At the limit (`max_drawdown`) everything is sold and the autopilot halts until Resume, which restarts the peaks from where the books stand. The books are marked with the account's latest prices, and the 10-minute check between runs marks them too, so a crash during the day closes the positions then instead of at the next 00:05. The books are about the plan, not the fills: what the fills cost is in the fill costs above. They are in `data/automation.json` and in `GET /systematic/automation` (`book`, `books`); switching the autopilot on starts them afresh.

| Endpoint | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `GET /systematic/overview` | strategies with research and checks, today's combined plan, equity, drawdown and status in one call |
| `GET`/`PUT /systematic/automation` | mode (`off`, `dry-run`, `paper`), `automatic` and `crypto` (or an explicit `strategies` list, which means manual), share of equity, drawdown stop (`max_drawdown`) and size cut (`reduce_at`), and the scanner `sleeve` (off by default; see [scanner.md](scanner.md)); a change deploys at once |
| `GET /systematic/execution` | every settled autopilot order with its fill, the price the backtest assumed, the cost and the shortfall, and the cost per asset class against the backtest's |
| `POST /systematic/automation/resume` | clear a drawdown halt |
| `POST /systematic/automation/run` | run the configured rebalance now |

## Managing strategies

Strategies are data, not code. Each one is a row of `platform.strategy_definitions` and its versions are rows of `platform.strategy_definition_versions`, written by `features/strategies/` (`/strategies` in the API, the Strategies page in the UI). The rules that turn a definition into weights (`signals.py`: average, Donchian, momentum, multi-speed trend) stay code, so a definition only chooses among vetted building blocks.

- **Templates.** On start the API adds each built-in template the database has never had (`catalog.seed`). A template you change or archive is left as you made it.
- **Versions.** Saving never changes a version: it adds the next one, and the history says what changed in each. The lifecycle sees a new version and starts it again at research, so an edit cannot trade untested. A version that changes only the wording (title, description, note) under the same code keeps its stage, its days in it and the decisions it logged. The trading node builds each strategy from the version the lifecycle promoted (`strategy_versions.params`), never from the current definition.
- **Checks on save.** Every symbol must pass the Shariah screen; a strategy holds coins or stocks, not both; the lookbacks, holdings, volatility and trading speed must fit the signal; the cost of a trade has a floor (10 bps for coins, 1 bp for stocks); only US equity funds may use the 1926 market proxy. The days in a year are derived, never entered.
- **Trials.** A trial is a distinct rule: the fields that change its returns (`catalog.rule_hash`), never its wording. Every rule ever saved or backtested as a draft counts once (`platform.strategy_trials`, `STRATEGIES.trials()`), archived ones included, so trying rules until one passes is counted against it, and renaming a rule or saving a copy of one adds nothing.
- **Before saving.** The editor checks the draft as it is typed (`POST /strategies/check`, nothing saved or counted): each symbol's Shariah verdict and days of price history against what the backtest needs, what is still invalid, any strategy with the very same rule, and what this account's own fills have cost against the cost the draft assumes. A draft can be backtested without saving it (`POST /strategies/preview`): the same backtest and five checks as a saved strategy, with the trial counted if the rule is new.
- **Archive, never delete.** An archived strategy leaves every list, backtest and lifecycle check; it cannot be archived while it is at shadow, canary or live, or while the autopilot has it selected. Restore brings it back.
- **Import and export.** A `.strategy.json` file (`format: "algotr.strategies"`, `format_version: 1`, schema at `GET /strategies/schema`) holds one or many definitions and, for reference, the metrics they had. An import is all or nothing, checked like a save, lands in research with no capital and never trusts the metrics in the file; a name already taken is refused, or imported as a numbered copy on request.

Every change is in the audit log (`strategy-create`, `-update`, `-archive`, `-restore`, `-import`).

`src/app/features/systematic/`, with the pieces other features share in `src/app/core/` and `src/app/quant/` (see [architecture.md](architecture.md)).
