# Terminal (React)

The trading terminal is a React and TypeScript single-page app in the
separate **algotr-frontend** repo. Its own nginx image serves it at `/ui`
and proxies every other path, and `/stream`, to the API (see the `frontend` service
in `docker-compose.yml`); the API itself serves no web pages. It replaced
the Streamlit dashboard on 7 November 2026.

| Command (in the algotr-frontend repo) | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `pnpm build` | type check + Vite build of `dist/`, served under `/ui` by nginx |
| `pnpm dev` | Vite's dev server on `http://localhost:5173/ui/`, proxying the API on port 8000 |
| `pnpm check` | Biome, the type check and the Vitest suite |
| `pnpm types` | regenerate `src/api/schema.d.ts` from the running API's OpenAPI schema |

**Stack.** Blueprint 6 for controls and layout, AG Grid Community for tables (sorting, filters, CSV export), TradingView Lightweight Charts for price and equity, ECharts for the remaining charts, TanStack Query with `openapi-react-query` for data, typed from the API's own schema with `openapi-typescript`, and Biome and Vitest. Packages come from the public npm registry (`frontend/.npmrc`; the user-wide registry is not used) and pnpm refuses any release younger than 14 days (`pnpm-workspace.yaml`). TypeScript stays on 5.9: TypeScript 7 no longer ships the compiler API that `openapi-typescript` uses.

**Shell.** A sidebar of views, a top bar (PAPER or LIVE, account value, today's change, the autopilot's drawdown, the kill switch, alerts and the theme), and a footer with the API's latency, the stream, the broker, the last quote and the market session. The kill switch asks for a reason and the typed word HALT, then sells what the autopilot holds and halts it (`POST /systematic/automation/halt`). Live quotes and order updates arrive over the `/stream` WebSocket; an order update reloads orders, positions and the account and shows a toast for a fill or a rejection. Dark by default, light on demand, both from Blueprint's design tokens with the terminal's amber accent and the bundled IBM Plex fonts.

**Order book** (coins: Alpaca has no stock book). Depth chart, ladder at 5 to 50 levels, imbalance at 5, 10 and 25 levels, order flow over 1 and 5 minutes, spread against its usual level, depth within 10 bps, the last trade, and the last 30 minutes of imbalance and spread.

**Strategies.** A table of every rule and the agent ensemble with type, status (paper, paused, passing n of 10 days, failed checks), Sharpe, CAGR, drawdown and checks. A strategy's page has its growth against buy and hold, CAGR, Sharpe, Sortino, Calmar, drawdown, win rate, profit factor and average holding of each invested stretch (`GET /systematic/strategies/{name}/stats`), its capital in the latest plan, the five checks with the long-history evidence, its fixed parameters, its recent signals, the event-driven check and Pause or Resume (`paused` in the autopilot's settings, which selection skips). Parameters are read-only and promotion is the gates' hysteresis, not a button.

**Signals and XAI.** The drift state as a banner with each stream's distance from its baseline. A feed of every agent decision as a signal: the weight chosen for each fund and its change from the day before (Buy or Sell beyond 2 points, else Hold); these models choose weights, so there is no separate confidence. A decision opens its SHAP waterfall from the average weight to the one chosen, a table of each feature's standardised value, push and percentile among recent decisions (from 10 of them), what the model relies on across its training days, a plain sentence built from the largest pushes, and Agree or Flag for review, written to the audit log (`signal-feedback`).

**Backtesting.** A form for one rule family (trend or dual momentum) over a grid of windows and, for momentum, funds held, with an optional Nautilus check of the best; runs go to a background queue (`POST /systematic/backtests`, last 50 kept in `data/backtests.json`). A finished run shows the best variant's growth against buy and hold and its five checks, every variant with its deflated Sharpe, a Sharpe heatmap over the parameters, five walk-forward folds, its trades, and the bias controls applied (look-ahead, adjusted prices, costs, multiple testing, survivorship, purge and embargo, event-driven validation). Every variant is logged as a `sweep` and counts as a trial from then on, which raises the deflated Sharpe's bar for every rule, including the ones trading; repeating a variant already tried adds nothing. Promotion stays with the gates: rules are fixed, so a variant never trades.

**Risk.** The kill switch's five steps with today's thresholds; value at risk at 95% and 99% over 1 and 5 days, historical, normal and expected shortfall, on a year of today's holdings (`GET /risk/summary`); every limit with its value, threshold, use and when it last refused an order (account limits are measured now, order limits on each buy); exposure by GICS sector against the 40% sector cap, by strategy book and by position against the 25% position limit; the correlation between holdings; and the risk events (refusals, kill switch halts, adverse drift).

**Compliance.** The share of holdings that pass the Shariah screen, wash sales this month, the year's purification and a note that pattern day trading no longer applies; every holding and listed name with its verdict, authority and purification ratio, with Re-screen all (a scanner refresh); the purification statement and the wash-sale log, each exportable as CSV.

**Dashboard, Positions and Orders.** Dashboard (summary, account value against SPY with the drawdown below it and a marker per day and side of fills, positions, the activity feed from `GET /activity`, and a rail with the kill switch's step, risk use, the running strategies and the status checks); Positions (live prices, Shariah verdict, days held, a detail drawer with lots by cost basis method, trades, headlines and, for coins, the order book, and closing several positions behind a typed confirmation); Orders (open orders with change and cancel, history with filters and CSV export, a lifecycle drawer with the slices of an algorithm order and, for a sell, the lots it closes under the specific-lot method, and cancel all behind a typed confirmation). Positions ends with the ledger against Alpaca: Sync, and Match Alpaca for a symbol that differs.

**Order ticket.** From any view, or Buy and Sell on Markets with the symbol filled in. Direct orders are market, limit, stop, stop-limit or trailing stop (by percent), with no exits, a bracket, one-triggers-other or one-cancels-other. An iceberg shows part of a limit order at a time; TWAP, VWAP and implementation shortfall spread the order over minutes, with the schedule previewed (`POST /orders/algo/preview`). The limit is filled from the latest price and every pre-trade check runs as the order is typed (`POST /orders/check`), so a refusal shows before anything is sent.

**Markets.** The watchlist with live mid prices, a TradingView-style chart (KLineChart, in `components/chart/`): a bar of timeframes (1m to 1D), an indicator search (`Alt+I`, 26 built-in indicators with a one-line explanation each; the legend's eye, sliders and cross hide, edit the lengths of, or remove one), candles, hollow candles, bars or area with an optional blue and orange colour-blind palette, linear, log (`Alt+L`) or percent (`Alt+P`) price scale, reset view (`Alt+R`), save as PNG and a full window (`Alt+Enter`, or `Esc` while the chart has focus; it is the page, not the browser's full screen, so tooltips and menus keep working). The left bar holds ten drawing tools (`Alt+T` trend line, `Alt+H`, `Alt+V`, `Alt+F`), a magnet (`Alt+M`), hide and remove; `Esc` cancels a tool, `Delete` removes the selected drawing (only while the chart has focus, so it never reaches the order shortcut), and drawings are kept per symbol in the browser. Every control has a tooltip with its name, a line of explanation and its shortcut, both bars are keyboard toolbars (arrow keys, `Home`, `End`), and the layout is remembered in the browser, the scanner's verdict on the symbol check by check, its latest headlines with the FinBERT score, the night's scan with its funnel and candidates, and the crypto table. A row of either table charts it. **Live bars.** Every trade prints into the open bar and opens the next one at the period boundary (`components/liveBars.ts`). Any symbol you chart streams: the chart tells the API which symbols it shows over `/stream` (`{"action":"watch","symbols":[...]}`, five per client, `WATCH_LIMIT` in all, default 20 on top of `STREAM_SYMBOLS`; Alpaca's free plan allows 30 per connection). The API hands the set to the trading node on the `market_watch` channel every 30 seconds; the node subscribes once the instrument loads and drops a symbol 90 seconds after the last ask. With the node down the API streams them itself. The latest bars are also polled (5 s off the stream, 15 s on it), and a polled bar replaces the live one only when it has at least as much volume. A badge shows Live or Polled. **Overlays.** The Overlay menu in the chart bar adds what happened behind the chart. *My trades* marks every ledger fill for the symbol (`/ledger/fills`, a year, 500 at most) as a triangle placed on the bar it happened in (fills on one bar and side merge into one marker at their average price); hovering shows the price, the cost report's arrival price, seconds to fill and shortfall (`/tca`, 90 days) and what the lots it closed made (`/ledger/realized`). *A rule* (the strategies that trade the symbol, from `/systematic/strategies`) replays the backtest on the daily chart: `GET /systematic/strategies/{name}/trace?symbol=&years=` recomputes the rule day by day with the code the backtest runs (`signals.weights`, shifted a day, filled at the open or the close as the rule does) and returns candles, its readings (the average, or the Donchian high and midpoint, the votes, the volatility scale, the momentum gain), the weight held and each holding stretch with its entry, exit and price gain. The chart draws the readings on the candles, shades the days the rule held, puts the weight in its own pane, marks entries and exits, and the table below lists the trades; a click zooms to one. Each entry and exit says why: what the rule read on the close it decided on (`why_entry`, `why_exit`). A status line names the rule, the day evaluation starts, the fill convention and the fee, and picks 1, 3, 5 or 8 years. A stretch already open when the backtest's window starts is entered at that bar, because the backtest earns nothing before it. The trades compound to the backtest's total return at zero fees (tested against `backtest.run`, and checked on real BTC/USD history: 0.906118 both ways).

**Research.** Rule lab (each family's variants with Sharpe, CAGR, drawdown, t, deflated Sharpe and survival; Run lab adds the variants to the trial count), Agents (the agents followed, their ensemble weights and the latest judgement by the gates, run by `poe ai-evaluate`), FinRL (the latest run and its judgement; Run FinRL takes about 15 minutes) and Scanner (variants on history against the benchmark, shadow picks' excess over SPUS at 1, 5 and 21 days, and the sleeve's criteria).

**Audit log.** Every entry with its kind and payload, the hash chain's state, a filter by kind, the full payload of a chosen entry and CSV export.

**System.** This API's latency (p50, p95 and p99 over 15 minutes, server errors, slowest routes, from a timing middleware), a timed call to Alpaca every 30 seconds with its error rate, each Alpaca stream with its reconnects and errors (counted per stream thread from alpaca-py's own log lines), each data feed's age, the sidecar and the agents' training and next retrain, storage sizes, and the order path: our round trip including the risk checks, Alpaca's acceptance and the time to fill (`GET /system/health`). Below that the status checks and every scheduled job with its next run (`GET /system/jobs`). The sidebar counts failing checks.

**Settings.** Risk limits (largest position, daily loss stop, share of daily volume, widest spread, largest sector, correlation warning, coin book imbalance) and the autopilot (mode up to paper, engine, allocation, drawdown, reduce point, automatic choice, crypto, scanner sleeve), each saved after a review of what changes and written to the audit log as `settings-change` with what it replaced (`PUT /settings/limits`, `DELETE` returns to the environment's values). Alert channels per kind of event, the webhook URL and a test alert. The universes, read-only, and the time zone for times shown.

**Alerts.** The alert center (`features/alerts`) watches the audit log and status every 30 seconds and the order stream as it arrives: fills, rejected or failed orders, orders refused by a risk check, the kill switch, a failed autopilot run, drift and a status check turning to error. Each goes in the app (the bell and a toast), by webhook (a JSON POST that Slack and Discord accept) or by email (SMTP from `.env`), as Settings says. The kill switch, failed runs and rejected orders go to every configured channel and cannot be muted.


**Command center** (the landing view at `/ui/`). Daily P&L, open positions, active strategies and the risk state as four tiles; the live activity feed; the agents' latest Buy and Sell moves, each opening its SHAP explanation in a drawer; and Alpaca's connection: each stream, the last tick, the median order round trip and the fill rate of the last 100 orders (`GET /system/connection`). A recovery bar appears above them whenever the kill switch is past normal or the autopilot is halted: why, what the last run did, the steps to recover and Resume behind a typed confirmation. The former landing view is at `/ui/dashboard`.

**Reconciliation.** Every symbol in the ledger or at Alpaca side by side: quantities and their difference, average costs, unrealized P&L each way, and a state (in sync, fee pending, cost differs, quantity differs, orphan held only by the ledger, zombie held only at Alpaca). Correct now adopts Alpaca's quantity with an audited adjusting lot or write-off. The banner gives the last check, what triggered it (the 10-minute schedule, a fill or a hand sync) and the newest Alpaca activity stored (`GET /ledger/comparison`).

**Freshness.** Each feed reports its age against a threshold for how often it should update: coin quotes 30 seconds, stock quotes a minute while the market is open, news 10 minutes, the ledger 20 minutes, the crypto table 36 hours, the scan one session (`GET /system/freshness`). The footer shows a dot per feed. Any stale feed, or a lost terminal stream, raises a banner on every view and fades the rows of positions whose quote is stale; the last data stays on screen.

**Paper and live.** The environment badge is amber for PAPER and red for LIVE, and repeats in the ticket. In paper the ticket carries a SIMULATION watermark; on a live account it shows REAL CAPITAL and asks for the quantity to be typed again, the quick order bar opens the ticket instead of sending, and the page gets a red edge. Automation stays paper only, so there is no paper-against-live view; Strategies compares paper with the backtest instead.

**Keyboard.** `Ctrl+K` or `/` opens a command palette (a symbol to chart or trade, any view, New order, Cancel every order, Kill switch); `?` shows every shortcut. `B` and `S` open the ticket on the focused symbol (the last one charted or clicked), `Del` cancels the selected order, `Shift+Del` cancels every order after confirming, `F` closes the selected positions after confirming, `G` then `C`, `D`, `T`, `M`, `P`, `O`, `R` or `S` moves to Command center, Dashboard, the order book, Markets, Positions, Orders, Risk or Settings, and `Esc` twice opens the kill switch.

**Order book.** A click on a ladder price opens the ticket as a limit at that price (buy at an ask, sell at a bid), a click on a size also fills the quantity, and a right click offers buy, sell or a price alert at that level. A quick order bar above the ladder takes side, quantity and an optional limit and sends on Enter.

**Ticket estimates.** For an order that would trade now: half the quoted spread, temporary impact by the square-root law (volatility times the square root of the share of daily volume, with the constant between 0.5 and 1.5) or, for a coin, the walk through the live book, and permanent impact from the Almgren and Chriss parameters the shortfall algorithm uses. For a resting limit: the chance a random walk with the symbol's volatility touches it in the rest of the session (`POST /orders/estimate`). Under it, value at risk, the symbol's sector, the largest position and the highest correlation now and after the order (`POST /risk/what-if`).

**Trade blotter.** Every filled order in 7, 30 or 90 days against its decision price (the autopilot's), the open of its arrival minute, the TWAP of its life and the day's VWAP: timing, impact, shortfall and benchmark costs in basis points, coin fees, and totals by source (autopilot, algorithm, iceberg, by hand), algorithm and symbol, with CSV export (`GET /tca`). Alpaca does not report a fill's venue, so there is no venue breakdown.

**Prediction outcomes** (on Signals and XAI). Whether each agent's weight moves of 2 points or more and each FinBERT headline scoring 0.2 or more either way were right the next trading day, over 30, 90 and 365 days and in trending, ranging and volatile markets (from SPUS), FinBERT's calibration curve, and each agent's SHAP importance rank over 30, 60 and 90 days with its movement (`GET /systematic/outcomes`). The agents output weights, not confidences, so only FinBERT has a calibration curve.

**Risk storyboard** (on Risk). The risk events of the last day, three days or week on a timeline, the use of each limit sampled every five minutes (`GET /risk/history`, kept two weeks) against the limit line, and near misses: orders that passed using 80% or more of a limit (`GET /risk/near-misses`).

**Strategies.** Promotion readiness for every rule and the ensemble: stage, gates passed, the days-in-a-row bar towards the 10 that promote or retire it, and what happens next; and the paper fills' cost against what the backtest assumes. A strategy's page adds its paper value against its own backtest over the same days, from a daily log of each book's value (`GET /systematic/strategies/{name}/tracking`).

**Alerts.** P0 (kill switch, failed runs, rejected orders, a stream down for 30 seconds, the ledger disagreeing with Alpaca, a stalled event loop or an overdue job) goes everywhere and cannot be muted; P1 (risk refusals, drift, status errors, a limit at 75%, an anomaly, price alerts) goes in the app and by email; P2 (fills) in the app only. Any non-critical kind can be muted for a symbol for an hour from the bell. The watchdog runs on its own thread and alerts when the event loop has not beaten for a minute, skipping the gap when the laptop sleeps. The anomaly alert fires when a day's gain is more than three deviations above the last 30 days. Reported conditions are kept on disk, so a restart does not repeat them.

**Loading and errors.** Panels show skeleton rows while loading, an error with Retry when a request fails (a failing Alpaca call shows its message rather than an endless Loading), and a plain sentence when empty.

**Design tokens.** Colours (`--gain`, `--loss`, `--warn`, `--paper`, `--live`, `--page`, `--panel`, `--rule`, `--muted`), fonts (`--font-sans` IBM Plex Sans, `--font-mono` IBM Plex Mono with tabular figures), spacing (`--space-1` to `--space-8`, 4 to 32 px), `--radius` 2 px and `--z-ticket-button` are on `:root` in `theme.css`, with dark values under `.bp6-dark`; overlays use Blueprint's own layers.

**Refinements from the detailed description (7 November 2026).** The command center is two columns: the four vital tiles (each a link: Daily P&L tinted by sign with an intraday sparkline, open positions with net exposure, strategies running, paused and in error, and the risk state) over the activity feed, whose rows are coloured by outcome and open a drawer; the agent signals with the ensemble's PPO, SAC and A2C weights as a bar over the Alpaca panel, which adds the last heartbeat and orders submitted today and turns amber when stale and red when a stream is down. Reconciliation pins the worst drift first, lists orphans and zombies apart, names the affected symbols in its banner and says how long since the last fetch. Feeds in the footer show their age as text. The stale banner can be acknowledged on a paper account only. Unacknowledged P0 alerts stay in a strip at the top of every view until acknowledged, with links to where to look (the anomaly alert links Reconciliation and the Trade blotter). The anomaly alert is P0 and its copy asks for the state to be verified; three rejected orders in ten minutes raise a rejection-spike P0; configuration changes and model retrains are P2. LIVE carries a lock; the ticket shows REAL CAPITAL beside Submit and accepts the quantity or CONFIRM; every fill in the blotter, the order history and a position's trades carries the environment. The recovery bar shows only in the critical and kill states, names the trigger and its threshold, and appears on the dashboard too. Buttons with a shortcut show it in a tooltip, and the palette also flattens, cancels, buys and sells. A ladder price or size opens a small ticket anchored to the row; dragging across depth bars opens a TWAP ticket for that much; your working orders sit on their level and can be dragged to another (at once on paper, after confirming on a live account); right-click can copy the price. The quick bar takes a symbol with suggestions and, on a live account, the confirmation inline. The blotter shows each cost over the last seven days against the window with a daily sparkline (amber when worse), fees in dollars, a drawer per trade with its prices and slices, algorithm by symbol, and printing to PDF. Outcomes flag a source whose 30-day hit rate is 10 points from its 365-day one and list the last 50 outcomes together. The what-if shows each change and its cap. Sizing by shares shows the effect on sector, position and correlation against their limits. The tracking chart draws the backtest dashed and the gap as green or red bars, with a warning past 10%. Promotion shows each gate, days in the stage and the date of the next promotion if the gates keep passing. The storyboard adds a sparkline per limit against its line, an event list and near misses sorted by closeness. Errors name their class (timeout, authentication, network, server) with Retry and a link to System health. The candle chart moves its last bar with live trades, drawn once per frame.

**Market regimes.** A three-state hidden Markov model of SPUS daily returns (statsmodels' Markov-switching regression, a mean and a variance per state, fitted by maximum likelihood with ten random starts, `app/quant/regimes.py`). The state with the highest variance is volatile, the higher mean of the other two trending, the last ranging. Each day takes its most likely state from the filtered probabilities, which use returns up to that day only; the parameters are fitted on the whole history. Signals shows today's state, each state's probability, mean, volatility and typical stay, and the last 120 sessions as a strip, and judges accuracy by regime with it.

**Agent confidence.** At every decision the sidecar draws 256 actions from the agent's own stochastic policy (Gaussian for PPO and A2C, squashed Gaussian for SAC), turns each into weights the way the agent does, and records for every weight the share of draws that make the same move as the chosen weights (a buy, a sell or a hold within 2 points). It is kept with the agent's paper record from 7 November 2026 on (`GET /systematic/agents/{algorithm}/confidence`), shown as a badge on each signal (green from 0.8, amber from 0.6) and given its own calibration curve, which fills in as decisions are judged; earlier decisions were made by models no longer kept, so they have none.

**Phone.** Below 700 px wide the terminal shows only the command center, the dashboard, positions and signals, plus alerts and the kill switch; ordering, strategy and settings views say they are left to the desktop, and order buttons are hidden.

**Not built.** Session locking (asked to skip), the AI assistant (left out by choice), a venue breakdown and Alpaca's rate-limit headroom (Alpaca reports neither), Server-Sent Events for fills (Alpaca offers them on the Broker API only; the account stream is a WebSocket backed by the ledger's polling), paper-against-live comparison (automation is paper only), and multi-account views.
